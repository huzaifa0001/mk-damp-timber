Wet rot

Wet rot occurs where timber remains damp for extended periods due to leaks, poor ventilation or water ingress. It causes timber to soften and can rapidly reduce structural strength if untreated.

Signs

  • Soft, spongy timber
  • Dark staining and a musty smell
  • Blistering or peeling paint in affected areas

Dry rot

Dry rot is caused by specific fungi that can transport moisture, allowing decay to spread from damp to otherwise dry timber. It can be more destructive and often requires careful removal and structural repairs.

Signs

  • Fruiting bodies (mushroom-like growths)
  • Timber becomes dry, brittle and cracked
  • Orange/rust-coloured spores and a strong musty smell
